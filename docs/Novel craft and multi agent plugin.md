# Build the novel plugin outline-first, judge by evidence

A good novel is a validated structure (a disruption by ~12%, an irreversible commitment by ~25%, a midpoint reversal, a death of the old belief at ~75%, a climax that answers the question the hook posed) carried by a protagonist whose Want, Need, Lie and Ghost embody the book's thematic argument, delivered in scenes that each turn a value, and written in prose that controls psychic distance and refuses to explain what it has already shown. AI-written fiction fails on exactly the layer that matters most: the research now shows narrative construction alone identifies AI stories at **93.2% macro-F1** ([StoryScope](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03136)), while word-level tells like "delve" fade within a model generation and fool neither experts nor modern detectors. The fix is therefore structural before lexical: subplots, morally compromised protagonists, unresolved threads, non-linear moves, plainly named emotions instead of another clenched jaw, and rate-limited (not banned) rhetorical habits. Turned into a Claude Code plugin, this means a file-first design in which the book bible, outline and scene manifest are the only source of truth; an orchestrator skill sequences specialist subagents through outline-first stages; drafting runs sequentially per POV thread from retrieval-composed context packs; critique runs as a parallel panel of blind, evidence-citing reviewers whose output is diffs the author accepts or rejects; genre knowledge ships as one-level-deep reference files, with mystery (and its Nordic/Finnish variant) the deepest; and hooks enforce the mechanical invariants. Every author checkpoint is a stage boundary where agents write files and stop, because subagents cannot ask the user questions and workflows cannot pause mid-run ([Workflows](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/workflows)). The evidence-backed priority is clear: invest in the outline layer and the drafter's handoff from outline to scene, because outline removal caused the largest quality drop in ablations (**4.3 to 2.5** in [StoryWriter](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16445)) while planning agents without a capable writer "performed poorly" ([Agents' Room](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02603v2)).

## Every structure framework converges on one beat map

The craft literature disagrees loudly about names and quietly agrees about shape. Three-act, Save the Cat, Weiland, Dan Wells's Seven-Point, Story Grid and the Hero's Journey all allocate roughly **25/50/25** across setup, confrontation and resolution ([Aliventures](https://www.aliventures.com/three-act-structure/); [Story Grid](https://storygrid.com/414/)), and they place the same load-bearing beats at the same percentages: an inciting disruption around **10–12%**, a "doorway of no return" at **~25%**, a midpoint at **50%** where new information flips the protagonist from reacting to acting, a lowest point at **~75%** where "a cherished lie dies," and a climax around **85–90%** ([Janet's Writing Blog on Weiland](https://janetswritingblog.com/2022/01/17/trying-to-get-novel-structure-right/); [Reedsy Save the Cat beat sheet](https://reedsy.com/blog/guide/story-structure/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/)). Weiland's pinch points at **~37% and ~62%** add a requirement most writers skip: the antagonist's force must be *seen on the page*, not reported ([Writers Helping Writers](https://writershelpingwriters.net/2021/10/what-are-pinch-points-and-where-do-they-go/)). Wells's practical rule, plan the Resolution first and make the Hook its opposite, gives a reviewer a cheap test for whether an arc exists at all ([Writing Excuses 7.41](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-7-41-seven-point-story-structure/)). Story Grid scales this to word counts: a 100,000-word novel runs **~25k/50k/25k** across 50–60 scenes of roughly 2,000 words ([Story Grid](https://storygrid.com/414/)). Two deliberate alternatives exist and must be recognised rather than "fixed": kishōtenketsu, where tension comes from wondering how elements connect and the payoff is understanding rather than winning ([StorytellingDB](https://storytellingdb.com/kishotenketsu/)), and Jane Alison's meanders, spirals and explosions ([Riot Material](https://www.riotmaterial.com/meander-spiral-explode-design-and-pattern-in-narrative/)). A structural reviewer should ask which model the author is using before diagnosing "missing conflict."

Below the act level, the two dominant scene models agree that a scene earns its place only if a character pursues something, meets opposition and ends in a different state. Swain's Scene (Goal, Conflict, Setback) and Sequel (Reaction, Dilemma, Decision) ([Advanced Fiction Writing](https://www.advancedfictionwriting.com/blog/2009/05/25/scenes-sequels-and-chapters/)) and Story Grid's Five Commandments (inciting incident, progressive complications culminating in a turning point, crisis as best-bad-choice or irreconcilable goods, climax as action, resolution) ([Story Grid](https://storygrid.com/5-commandments-storytelling-revisited/)) both reduce to one test: can you state the value shift "from X to Y" and point to the sentence where the character changes tactics? If not, "the passage is exposition with nothing happening" ([Story Grid, Value Shift 101](https://storygrid.com/value-shift-101/)). "In late, out early" governs the edges: cut from the start until the scene breaks, leave as soon as the goal is achieved ([The Story Department](https://www.thestorydepartment.com/screenwriting-in-late-out-early/)). This test is directly machine-checkable in a scene manifest that requires `goal`, `conflict`, `turn` and `outcome` fields per scene, which is why the plugin design below validates the outline with a script before any drafting begins.

Character and theme are the same tool viewed from two sides. Weiland's Lie/Truth pair, Truby's weakness-and-need, Cron's misbelief and Egri's premise all describe a protagonist whose conscious Want conflicts with an unconscious Need because of a false belief rooted in a wound ([Shortform on Weiland](https://www.shortform.com/pdf/creating-character-arcs-pdf-k-m-weiland); [StorytellingDB on Story Genius](https://storytellingdb.com/story-genius-method-lisa-cron/)). The antagonist should want the *same object* (Truby), be as strong as the protagonist (Egri), and embody "the path your Protagonist could take—but must resist" ([Story Grid](https://storygrid.com/protagonist-and-antagonist/)). McKee's controlling idea makes theme a cause-and-effect sentence proven by the climax, and his counter-idea rule is the antidote to preachiness: each scene's value change proves either the theme or its opposite, and if the counter-idea never wins a scene the book is one-sided ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/what-is-a-controlling-idea/)). A character audit therefore reads: Want / Need / Lie / Truth / Ghost / what they do at 25% they cannot undo / what they learn at 50% / what dies at 75% / the choice at the climax that proves the arc. Any blank is a structural gap, and the Lie should equal the counter-idea so the arc *is* the argument.

At line level the consensus is equally firm. Gardner's five-level psychic-distance ladder should move deliberately, not jump ([The Strangest Times](https://thestrangesttimes.substack.com/p/psychic-distance)); filter words (saw, heard, felt, noticed, realized) should be cut in close POV ([Writers in the Storm](https://writersinthestormblog.com/2016/10/how-filtering-the-point-of-view-affects-show-dont-tell/)); dialogue should carry subtext and use "said" ([Palomar Pressbooks](https://pressbooks.palomar.edu/candacerose/chapter/chapter-12-dialogue/)); and Browne & King's fourteen self-editing checks, King's "second draft = first draft minus 10%" and Le Guin's "only the relevant belongs" all say the same thing about padding ([Fiction Foundry](https://fictionfoundry.alumni.columbia.edu/seffw); [Signal v. Noise](https://signalvnoise.com/posts/322-excerpts-from-stephen-kings-on-writing); [Radacts](https://radacts.substack.com/p/crowding-and-leaping)). Maass's distinction matters most for the AI problem: readers' emotion comes from themselves, so "action is an opportunity for us to feel something, not a cause of feeling something," and stated feelings fall flat unless fresh ([Jane Friedman](https://janefriedman.com/produce-emotional-response)). Pacing has one hard number: **46.4%** of readers in a Goodreads survey cited "slow and boring" as their top reason for abandoning a book ([Tyson Adams](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/4505032-why-are-books-abandoned)), which Maass answers with line-by-line micro-tension and the structure crowd answers with pinch points and try/fail cycles that escalate ("yes, but" / "no, and") rather than repeat ([Mythic Scribes](https://mythicscribes.com/interviews/donald-maass/); [Sanderson](https://brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/brandon-sandersons-2025-overview-of-story-structure-lecture-3)). Agents decide within **five to ten pages** and reject alarm clocks, mirrors, dreams, info-dumps and purple prose ([Renée Fountain](https://reneefountain.substack.com/p/the-science-of-rejection-how-agents)); endings must be surprising yet inevitable, follow an unbroken "because" chain, and show the character changed ([Writers.com](https://writers.com/?p=19837)). Revision runs structural → character → scene → line → copy, because fixing sentences in a scene that will be cut wastes the work ([Editor World](https://www.editorworld.com/article/how-to-revise-a-novel)). That ordering is the spine of the plugin's reviewer hierarchy.

## Structure, not vocabulary, is the AI fingerprint

The strongest research finding reverses the folk wisdom. Word lists are real but weak: Kobak et al. found "delves" at **28x** excess frequency in 14 million PubMed abstracts ([arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016v4)), and expert annotators named vocabulary as their top cue in 53% of explanations ([arXiv 2501.15654](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654v2)), but that lexicon (delve, tapestry, testament, underscore) is corporate and rarely appears in fiction narration, and it ages fast ("delve" faded in GPT-5-era output per [DETAILED_PATTERNS.md](https://tangled.org/secluded.site/agent-skills/blob/main/skills/humanize/references/DETAILED_PATTERNS.md?raw=1)). Em dashes are model-dependent: Russell et al. found AI text in their corpus *avoided* dashes ([arXiv 2501.15654](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654v2)). The durable signal is narrative. StoryScope, which compared 61,608 human and LLM stories across five frontier models, found narrators explicitly state the theme in **77% of AI stories vs 52% human**, emotion routed through bodily sensation clichés in **81% vs 38%**, explicit emotion labels in only **8% vs 29%**, no subplot in **79% vs 57%**, protagonist-driven resolution in 69% vs 46%, and morally ambivalent protagonists in **38% vs 59%** ([arXiv 2604.03136](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03136)). Narrative-only features detect AI fiction at **93.2% macro-F1**; the 30 strongest features alone reach 84.8%. Tian et al. found GPT-4's major setback and climax arrive measurably earlier so tension resolves too quickly, and the model used "Man in Hole" in over half its stories while producing "Riches to Rags" **1.3% vs 14.6%** for humans ([arXiv 2407.13248](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.13248v2)). Claude specifically shows "notably flat event escalation," the most uniform voice, a preference for epilogues and a "reverent, continuist" treatment of convention ([arXiv 2604.03136](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03136)); that profile is worth remembering when Claude is the drafter.

The single most counter-intuitive implication is that rigid "show, don't tell" is itself an AI tell. Humans name emotions plainly nearly four times as often as models do; the model's substitute is the jaw that tightens, the breath that hitches, the chest that constricts, followed within a sentence by the emotion noun anyway ([arXiv 2604.03136](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03136); [SleuthSayers](https://www.sleuthsayers.org/2026/02/what-ai-fundamentally-misunderstands.html)). Humanising sometimes means *telling* ("She was angry") rather than adding another clenched fist. The second implication is that scene endings carry a signature: the "wisdom-summary" sentence ("Some wounds, she understood now, never fully closed") appears "almost always" at section ends, as does "for now it would have to be enough" ([SleuthSayers](https://www.sleuthsayers.org/2026/02/what-ai-fundamentally-misunderstands.html); [r/WritingWithAI](https://axebps-redlib.hf.space/r/WritingWithAI/comments/1ld5e5d/list_of_weird_expressions_that_ai_constantly)), and a working novelist describes "coda overreach," scenes that run past their end explaining meaning because "the robot doesn't trust them" ([Jason Craft](https://www.jasoncraft.org/workbench/posts/03-style-engineering/)). Dialogue tells are consistent across sources: philosophical debate in **59% of AI stories vs 34%** ([arXiv 2604.03136](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03136)), therapy-speak, mic-drop closers, every character "the same eloquent, slightly formal person wearing different hats," and arguments that resolve too fast because the model softens conflict ([Dan Guru](https://danguru.substack.com/p/the-ai-dialogue-discovery); [voice thread](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qvkkha/snapshots/2026-02-06T18%3A11%3A41.924807Z)). Names are measurable and model-specific: across 20,000 stories, **Elias appeared in 26.5%**, Mara 16.7%, Elara 13.1%, "lighthouse" in 51.2%, and 56% of Claude's stories were titled "The Lighthouse Keeper's Secret"; Elias is ~900x more frequent than in contemporary novels ([arXiv 2605.26492](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26492v1); [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/333804)). A name list in the bible is the cheapest high-value fix in the entire system.

Sentence-level tells are real but must be rate-limited, not banned. The negation reframe ("not X, but Y") is a tell only when "the negation corrects no real assumption" the reader held ([rentry guide](https://rentry.co/ijustmadesomebulllshit)); practitioners report models producing "twenty per thousand words if you let it" and em-dash drafts "hitting 20+ per thousand" ([Jason Craft](https://www.jasoncraft.org/workbench/posts/03-style-engineering/)). Tricolons, participial tails, reflective gerund openers ("Watching him leave, she realized…"), "un-" adjective clusters, "the kind of X that," synonym cycling and metronomic 15–25-word sentences round out the list ([Wikipedia mirror](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/Wikipedia%3ASigns_of_AI_writing); [SleuthSayers](https://www.sleuthsayers.org/2026/02/what-ai-fundamentally-misunderstands.html)). Anthropic's own skill states "Structure is the #1 detection signal" and warns that "over-polishing: aggressively editing out every irregularity can push human writing toward AI statistical profiles" ([avoid-ai-writing SKILL.md, local copy]). Rob Burke's finding that his own pre-2023 prose scored 0.0–0.1 cliché hits per 100 words versus 0.4–5.8 for slop corpora, yet contained genuine colon-triples, argues for a per-author baseline rather than universal thresholds ([robburke.net](https://robburke.net/?p=4311)). The noslop project lists the over-correction failure modes that a careless de-AI pass creates: "anchor stuffing, fragment cosplay, signature closers, and punchy one-line morals" ([noslop README](https://github.com/vstaln/noslop)).

Countermeasures that work share a shape. Jason Craft found a style guide alone failed ("the model agreed with every rule and still produced therapy-speak"); what worked was a positive-constraint constitution loaded every session plus regex lint treated "like a code coverage report, not a pass/fail test," plus retrieval of curated exemplar passages, plus hand-edits promoted into permanent rules ([Jason Craft](https://www.jasoncraft.org/workbench/posts/03-style-engineering/)). This matches the research: adding explicit discourse features improved diversity, suspense and arousal by **>40%** ([arXiv 2407.13248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13248v2)) while negative prompting and temperature scaling "do not meaningfully reduce homogeneity" ([arXiv 2606.17350](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.17350)). Generate with positive constraints, revise with negative ones. The r/aigamedev detail that replacement clichés should be drawn randomly from sets of 3–4 so the fix does not form a new fingerprint ([r/aigamedev](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sevhey/snapshots/2026-04-09T20%3A33%3A34.459213Z)) is the kind of thing a script can enforce and a prompt cannot. Finally, continuity drift is a pipeline problem, not a prose problem: a 300k-word practitioner reports "by chapter 80, it's forgotten the specific cadence from chapter 12," eye colours changing and characters learning things too early ([long-novel snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1rb65g7/snapshots/2026-02-22T14%3A24%3A18.05425Z)), and no frontier model stayed stable beyond 64k tokens on one long-document benchmark ([arXiv 2603.13545](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.13545)). The fix is structured state (book bible, per-scene fact ledger, knowledge ledger per character), not editing.

The lint taxonomy the plugin should implement follows from this. **Always fix:** explained subtext, theme stated outright, emotion label stacked on a body cue, similes that do not map, chatbot artifacts, continuity contradictions, descriptor recycled verbatim, corporate lexicon inside narration. **Rate-limit per 1,000 words:** em dashes, "not X but Y," tricolons, fragments, one-line paragraphs, reflective gerund openers, un-adjectives, body-cliché emotion, "hung in the air," ozone and unnamed smells, similes, non-"said" tags, scene-closing wisdom lines. **Check at outline level:** subplot present, protagonist morally compromised somewhere, at least one resolution not caused by the protagonist, at least one unresolved thread, some non-linear move, climax not early, valence not uniformly positive, chapter shapes not identical, no default names. **Do not over-correct:** plain emotion words, comma splices, digressions and repeated clear words are human signals.

## Mystery is a fair-play contract; Nordic noir rewrites its ending

Every genre skill needs three layers: the contract (core value, emotion, ending promise), the obligatory scenes (checkable), and the dominant-genre rule for blends. Story Grid supplies the first two most explicitly, and the Writer's Digest rule supplies the third: in a compound label the second word names the base genre whose central question must be answered ([Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/get-published-sell-my-work/the-dos-and-donts-of-combining-genres)). Word counts converge at **80–100k** for most adult genres, SF/F at 100–115k, YA 55–80k, MG 20–55k, picture books 400–800 words, with a soft debut ceiling near 100–110k ([Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/how-many-words-in-a-novel); [Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/whats-new/word-count-for-novels-and-childrens-books-the-definitive-post)).

Mystery, the deepest skill, is a puzzle governed by rules that have barely moved since 1929. Knox's Decalogue and Van Dine's Twenty Rules reduce to one promise: the reader has an equal chance, the culprit is a prominent early character whose thoughts the reader has not followed, the detective actually detects, and the solution comes by logic, never by coincidence, unmotivated confession or a suspect introduced late ([Wikipedia, Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age_of_Detective_Fiction); [Van Dine](https://www.umsl.edu/~gradyf/film/vandinerules.htm)). The Detection Club oath still forbids solving crimes by "Divine Revelation, Feminine Intuition, Mumbo Jumbo, Jiggery-Pokery, Coincidence or Act of God" ([Wikipedia, Detection Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detection_Club)). Story Grid adds the obligatory moments: inciting crime, speech in praise of the villain, discovery of the MacGuffin, progressively complicated clue-following, exposure of the criminal and a brought-to-justice-or-escapes moment, with readers feeling "cheated if the criminal is never exposed" ([Story Grid, Crime](https://storygrid.com/crime-genre/)). The engineering is concrete: **three to seven suspects, five typical**, each with means, motive, opportunity, a lie (only one lies about the murder) and a non-murder secret ([Writers Write](https://www.writerswrite.co.za/?p=1292835); [Susan Spann](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/5042430-25-things-you-need-to-know-about-writing-mysteries-by-susan-spann)); each important clue planted **three times**, escalating from subtle to clear in hindsight, paired with two red herrings, buried fourth or fifth in a list because readers remember the first three and the last ([IF craft corpus](https://pvliesdonk.github.io/if-craft-corpus/corpus-docs/genre-conventions/mystery_conventions); [The Write Practice](https://thewritepractice.com/mystery-clues/)); a body by **about page 50**; a first solution reached by the midpoint that collapses into a low point; and a reveal that explains clues and motive and ends quickly, because a 20-page denouement is a common failure ([Susan Spann](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/5042430-25-things-you-need-to-know-about-writing-mysteries-by-susan-spann)). Reader complaints map precisely onto AI tendencies: culprit "picked out of a hat," unexplained red herrings, detectives withholding from the reader, too-easy confessions and loose ends ([SleuthSayers](https://www.sleuthsayers.org/2017/07/whodunits-pet-peeves.html)). The structural check for a mystery agent is therefore two timelines, the murderer's and the detective's ([Now Novel](https://nownovel.com/how-to-write-murder-mystery/)), plus a clue tracker that proves every clue was on the page before the reveal.

Subgenres change tone and tidiness, not the fair-play core. Cozies demand an amateur sleuth, a small community, off-page murder, no profanity or sex, a hobby hook and a tidy solution ([Writers Who Kill](https://writerswhokill.blogspot.com/2021/08/reader-contracts-by-v-m-burns.html); [Career Authors](https://careerauthors.com/formula-for-writing-a-cozy-mystery-part-4-murder/)). Hardboiled and noir invert that: "the quest matters more than the solution, which is often weak or contrived," and readers "should not expect tidy endings" ([Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/media/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/hard-boiled-detective-fiction); [IF craft corpus](https://pvliesdonk.github.io/if-craft-corpus/corpus-docs/genre-conventions/mystery_conventions)). The police procedural, dominant since the mid-1970s, replaces the lone genius with an ensemble, procedural realism and institutional limits, and fails on legal errors and "copaganda" ([Writeseen](https://writeseen.com/blog/police-procedural-crime-fiction)).

Nordic noir is a thematic overlay on the procedural, and its lineage explains its rules. Sjöwall and Wahlöö wrote the Martin Beck series (1965–75) from a Marxist perspective to dissect the welfare state; the genre since then "relocates Anglo-American conventions to a welfare society and challenges the order from within rather than reassuring" ([nordics.info](https://nordics.info/show/artikel/crime-fiction); [Stougaard-Nielsen](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10147649/1/Stougaard-Nielsen_Scandinavian%20Crime%20Fiction_pre-print.pdf)). Its conventions are checkable: the investigator's body indexes social dysfunction (Beck's stomach, Wallander's diabetes, Hole's alcoholism); the landscape and season act as antagonists; the crime is treated as a social function with a theme such as violence against women, child-welfare failure or xenophobia; the tone is "brooding, pessimistic," "rarely triumphant"; the prose is spare and unsentimental; and ambiguous endings are permitted ([Stougaard-Nielsen](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10147649/1/Stougaard-Nielsen_Scandinavian%20Crime%20Fiction_pre-print.pdf); [Scandification](https://scandification.com/nordic-noir-what-makes-scandinavian-crime-fiction-so-popular/)). The Finnish inflection is distinct enough to deserve its own reference section. Finnish crime fiction "has always" used humour and "entails many different shades on top of just black," against Swedish work that is "so depressing and sinister" ([CrimeReads](https://crimereads.com/finland-the-lighter-side-of-nordic-noir/)); it is "more hard-boiled," "good does not always win in the end," it carries "a certain Finnish craziness," more books run from the criminal's POV, dialogue is "realistic, simple and particularly important" because Finns are not talkative, winter is "an antagonist and source of conflict," and bodies under ice are revealed by the spring thaw ([finland.fi](https://finland.fi/?p=3414)). Lehtolainen's heroine "does not always follow the law, but knows her own moral values," and Joensuu's Harjunpää anchors the Finnish procedural roster ([finland.fi](https://finland.fi/?p=3414); [nordics.info](https://nordics.info/show/artikel/crime-fiction)). For a Finnish-crime skill, these become concrete checks: a laconic dialogue budget, a welfare-state fault line named in the premise, a seasonal clock (thaw, polar night, midsummer), humour that is permitted to break the gloom, a criminal-POV strand, and an ending that may expose without delivering justice. Every one of those also counters a measured AI tendency: flat valence, tidy endings, uniform voice and talkative characters.

| Literary / upmarket | Interiority, deliberate prose, moral grey, character change as the spine ([Nerd for Hire](https://write.as/nerd-for-hire/what-makes-fiction-literary)) | An intentional sentence-level style; ambiguity in judgement not in events; upmarket = one-line hook + one-sentence theme ([Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/upmarket-fiction/)) | "Quiet" = "lacking in narrative drive" to agents ([Ploughshares](https://pshares.org/blog/blurbese-quiet/)); even tone |
| Historical | ≥50 years past, from research; era-true values; author's note states changes ([HNS](https://historicalnovelsociety.org/defining-the-genre-what-are-the-rules-for-historical-fiction/); [Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/the-art-of-the-authors-note-honesty-magic-and-everything-you-dont-put-in-the-novel)) | "Cannot change" fact sheet; contemporary-neutral dialogue scrubbed of post-period idiom, stricter for the last ~150 years ([HNS, Gooden](https://historicalnovelsociety.org/language-historical-fiction-an-exploration-of-style-idiom-anachronism/)) | Modern-minded characters; research dumps |
| YA / MG / picture book | MG 8–12, hopeful, clean; YA 13–18, emotional immediacy, no adult retrospection, fade-to-black; picture book 32 pages, 500–700 words, child protagonist, no rhyme, no moral ([Writer's Digest](https://writersdigest.com/write-better-fiction/the-key-differences-between-middle-grade-vs-young-adult); [Authors.ai](https://authors.ai/tips-for-writing-young-adult-fiction/); [Alison Hughes](https://medium.com/@michaelhingston/picture-books-101-from-alison-hughes-9b9eec235b19)) | Reader reads up ~2 years; 14 spreads with page-turn hooks; voice over slang | Condescension, dated slang, easy answers; "overt morals are the kiss of death" |
| Erotica / romantic suspense | Erotica: sex is the engine, HEA optional; erotic romance: HEA required; romantic suspense: romance and suspense "equal partners," danger on page one, villain punished, couple happy ([All About Romance](https://allaboutromance.com/erotica-vs-erotic-romance-by-nan-de-plume/); [CrimeReads](https://crimereads.com/rules-for-writing-romantic-suspense/)) | Each sex scene changes the relationship; content warnings findable ([Counting Starsheep](https://countingstarsheep.com/a-rundown-of-the-dark-romance-genre-so-that-you-can-understand-why-trigger-warnings-matter/)); romantic suspense interleaves two beat sheets so romance beats land in breathing spaces ([Writer's Digest](https://www.writersdigest.com/write-better-fiction/the-balance-between-romance-and-romantic-suspense)) | "Twelve euphemisms for body parts," climax-skipping ([Sudowrite](https://sudowrite.com/blog/ai-erotica-prose-quality/)) |

For blends, the rule for a multi-agent system is: declare the base genre, load its full obligatory-scene list, load only the secondary genre's non-negotiables (romance → HEA and visible pairing; mystery → fair-play solution; horror → monster with rules), and resolve conflicts in favour of the base, except for recognised two-contract labels (romantasy, romantic suspense) where both lists are mandatory ([Uncanny Magazine](https://www.uncannymagazine.com/article/romantasy-all-along/); [Elizabeth Spann Craig](https://elizabethspanncraig.com/business-of-writing/reader-expectations-for-genre/)).

## Claude Code mechanics dictate a file-first, stage-gated design

The platform constraints are specific and verified against the live docs. A plugin is a directory with an optional `.claude-plugin/plugin.json` and conventional folders: `skills/` (one `<name>/SKILL.md` each, the recommended unit; `commands/` is legacy), `agents/` (Markdown files whose body is the system prompt), `hooks/hooks.json`, `workflows/` (JavaScript orchestration scripts), `scripts/`, `.mcp.json` ([Plugin manifest reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins-reference)). Three constraints shape everything else. First, **plugin agents ignore `hooks`, `mcpServers`, `permissionMode` and `initialPrompt`**, so every guardrail must be a plugin-level hook ([Plugin components](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins/components)). Second, **subagents cannot use `AskUserQuestion`**, see only their system prompt, the delegation message, preloaded skills and a sibling roster, and return only a summary ([Subagents](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)). Third, **workflows cannot pause for user input**: "For sign-off between stages, run each stage as its own workflow" ([Workflows](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/workflows)). Together these mean the author checkpoint has to be a stage boundary where agents write files and stop, and state must live on disk, which Anthropic's own multi-agent guidance endorses: "Subagents can store results externally and pass lightweight references back, avoiding information loss from repeated relaying" ([Multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

Several other mechanics are worth building around. Agent frontmatter supports `skills:` to preload up to 32 skills at startup, `tools:` allowlists, `model:`, `effort:`, `maxTurns:` and `memory:` ([Subagents](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)); skills support `user-invocable: false` for background knowledge, `disable-model-invocation: true` for side-effecting workflows, and `context: fork` for task skills (but "guideline-only skills return little useful output in a subagent") ([Skills](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills)). Anthropic's skill guidance says keep `SKILL.md` under 500 lines, keep references one level deep, use "one reference file per variant, so only the relevant one is read," prefer scripts for deterministic work, and write "pushy" third-person trigger descriptions ([Skill best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices); [skill-creator](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/skill-creator/SKILL.md)). The plan-validate-execute pattern ("have Claude write a `changes.json` plan, validate it with a script, then apply it") maps directly onto outline validation. Hooks give the mechanical enforcement layer: `PostToolUse` on `Write|Edit` for lint, `PreToolUse` for protecting canon files, `SessionStart` returning `additionalContext`, and `Stop`/`SubagentStop` with exit 2 to keep an agent working ([Hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)). Cost is not trivial: "multi-agent systems use roughly 15x" the tokens of chat, token usage explained ~80% of performance variance, and an Opus lead with Sonnet workers beat single Opus by 90.2% ([Multi-agent research system](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). Current pricing per MTok input/output is Fable 5.1 $10/$50, Opus 5.5 $4/$20, Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Haiku 5.5 from $0.10/$0.50, all with a 1M context window ([Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)).

Prior systems converge on the same skeleton and report the same failures. InkOS (~10.2k stars) separates Zod-validated JSON state from human-readable Markdown projections, treats its search index as "not a source of story facts," records audits as "observations rather than pass/fail verdicts," and judges completion "by tool results and files on disk, not by the model's claims"; its documented failure modes were continuity breaks, hook accumulation (40+ unresolved threads), context bloat ("$200+" per chapter before selective retrieval, "the single biggest improvement") and AI voice ([Narcooo/inkos](https://github.com/Narcooo/inkos); [dev.to InkOS write-up](https://dev.to/dylan_brown_4c803aefcfe51/building-an-autonomous-ai-agent-that-writes-novels-architecture-of-a-10-agent-pipeline-59pf)). ainovel-cli's lesson is "keep control flow deterministic and let only generation be non-deterministic," with every critique required to "cite the original text as evidence" ([dev.to ainovel-cli](https://dev.to/teum/this-go-cli-turns-one-sentence-into-a-500-chapter-novel-no-babysitting-required-i5m)). howells/fiction, a Claude Code plugin with 26 agents and persona critics, introduced `## ⚓ Anchored` headings "which reviewers treat as non-negotiable" ([howells/fiction](https://github.com/howells/fiction)). The Genesis 21-skill pipeline produced 68k- and 97k-word books that "both still needed human editing," with the core problem "voice convergence, where prose flattened after about 10,000 words" ([dev.to Genesis](https://dev.to/philipstark/i-built-21-ai-skills-that-write-a-full-book-from-one-sentence-4lgd)). Sudowrite's Story Bible reads up to 20,000 words of preceding text plus the bible, and its principle is that the AI "should know your story and support your voice, not replace either" ([Sudowrite](https://sudowrite.com/blog/how-sudowrite-works/)).

The academic record settles three design questions. Outline-first wins: DOC's hierarchical outliner beat Re3 on plot coherence 67.6% vs 45.1% ([DOC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10077)), StoryWriter's ablation showed removing the event outline caused the largest drop (**4.3 → 2.5**) ([StoryWriter](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16445)), and Dramatron's professional users edited plot outlines least ([Dramatron](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14958v1)). Sequential drafting within a thread wins: Dramatron originally generated scene dialogue in parallel and reverted after participants "found inconsistencies between scenes" ([Dramatron](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14958v1)). And absolute LLM judgement of creative quality is unreliable: on the Torrance Test of Creative Writing, LLM judges had **near-zero agreement with experts** (Cohen's κ ≈ 0.02–0.04) while New Yorker stories passed 84.7% of tests versus 8.7–30% for 2023 models ([Art or Artifice?](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.14556)); pairwise rubric-anchored judgement does better (Claude 3.7 Sonnet ~73% on LitBench, trained reward model 78%) but is length-biased, with 65% of preferred stories being longer ([LitBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00769v1)). Doshi and Hauser's warning completes the picture: AI ideas raised individual novelty but made stories **10.7% more similar** to each other, a "social dilemma" in which author selection among divergent options is where diversity re-enters ([University of Exeter](https://news.exeter.ac.uk/?p=13749)). Judges must therefore be pairwise, rubric-anchored, blind to provenance and length-debiased, and idea-generation stages must produce many options for the author to choose from rather than one "best."

## The recommended architecture: one plugin, one book tree, seven stages

**Plugin file tree.** The plugin ships knowledge, agents, scripts and hooks; it never holds book state, because `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}` changes on update ([Plugin manifest reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins-reference)).

```text
novelsmith/                                  # illustrative name; must not start with claude-/anthropic-
├── .claude-plugin/plugin.json               # name, version, userConfig: author_name, default_genre, target_words, voice_samples_dir
├── skills/
│   ├── novel-start/SKILL.md                 # scaffolds book/, interviews for premise, genre, voice samples (disable-model-invocation: true)
│   ├── bible/SKILL.md                       # create/update characters, world, timeline, name list; only route allowed to edit canon
│   ├── outline/SKILL.md                     # premise → acts → chapters → scenes.yaml; runs validate-outline.py until it passes
│   ├── draft/SKILL.md                       # /novelsmith:draft <scene|chapter>; sequential per POV thread
│   ├── critique/SKILL.md                    # /novelsmith:critique <chapter>; spawns blind parallel panel
│   ├── revise/SKILL.md                      # applies ONLY author-accepted notes as diffs; re-lints
│   ├── continuity/SKILL.md                  # facts-ledger vs bible sweep; knowledge-ledger violations
│   ├── status/SKILL.md                      # progress, open threads with admission dates, next action
│   ├── craft-structure/SKILL.md             # user-invocable: false; beat map, scene/sequel, value-shift test, endings
│   ├── craft-character/SKILL.md             # user-invocable: false; Want/Need/Lie/Ghost audit, antagonist mirror, orchestration
│   ├── craft-prose/SKILL.md                 # user-invocable: false; psychic distance, filters, R.U.E., dialogue subtext, Maass modes
│   ├── de-ai/SKILL.md                       # user-invocable: true; three-tier lint taxonomy + author voice calibration
│   │   └── references/{always-fix,rate-limit,outline-level,do-not-overcorrect}.md
│   └── genre/SKILL.md                       # one skill; genre-specialist reads only the file named in premise.md
│       └── references/
│           ├── mystery.md                   # fair play, clue economy, suspects, two timelines, subgenres (cozy, hardboiled, procedural)
│           ├── mystery-nordic-finnish.md    # welfare-state fault line, season as antagonist, laconic dialogue, humour, criminal POV, open endings
│           ├── thriller.md
│           ├── romance.md                   # + heat levels, dark-romance warnings, romantic-suspense interleave
│           ├── fantasy.md                   # Sanderson's laws, subgenre contracts
│           ├── science-fiction.md           # novum, Turkey City lint
│           ├── horror.md
│           ├── literary.md
│           ├── historical.md
│           ├── kidlit.md                    # YA, MG, picture book (14 spreads)
│           ├── erotica.md
│           └── blends.md                    # base-genre rule, two-contract labels
├── agents/
│   ├── story-architect.md                   # premise, promise, theme, 5+ divergent endings; model: opus; tools: Read, Write
│   ├── character-designer.md                # sheets + knowledge-ledger seeds + name list (no Elias/Mara/Elara); opus
│   ├── worldbuilder.md                      # rules, places, timeline; sonnet
│   ├── outliner.md                          # scenes.yaml with goal/conflict/turn/outcome; opus; tools incl. Bash(validate-outline.py)
│   ├── scene-drafter.md                     # ONE scene from a context pack; skills: [craft-structure, craft-character, craft-prose, genre, de-ai]; opus/fable; tools: Read, Write (manuscript only)
│   ├── line-editor.md                       # sentence-level diff proposals; sonnet; Read
│   ├── continuity-editor.md                 # facts vs bible, who-knows-what; haiku/sonnet; Read, Grep, Glob, Edit(ledger only)
│   ├── genre-specialist.md                  # obligatory scenes, clue fairness, beat placement; sonnet; Read
│   ├── de-ai-editor.md                      # tells, opener/rhythm monotony, cliché density vs per-author baseline; sonnet; Read, Bash(metrics)
│   ├── reader-simulator.md                  # blind persona reads; confusion, boredom, midpoint ending prediction; sonnet ×N
│   └── judge.md                             # pairwise, rubric-anchored, length-debiased, never sees provenance; opus
├── hooks/hooks.json
│   # PostToolUse  Write|Edit  → scripts/lint-scene.sh (script inspects tool_input path; acts only under book/manuscript/**)
│   # PreToolUse   Write|Edit  → scripts/guard-canon.sh (deny edits under book/bible/** unless CLAUDE_SKILL == bible; deny edits inside ## ⚓ Anchored)
│   # SessionStart             → scripts/status-summary.sh (additionalContext: next scene, open threads, last decision)
│   # SubagentStop             → scripts/check-output-files.sh (exit 2 if the agent claimed a write that is not on disk)
├── workflows/                               # optional; each stage is its own workflow because workflows cannot pause
│   ├── reader-panel.js                      # parallel(): N blind readers per chapter → judge dedupes and ranks
│   ├── continuity-sweep.js                  # pipeline(): per-chapter extract-facts → merge → diff vs bible
│   └── draft-act.js                         # pipeline() over scenes in manifest order (sequential by design)
├── scripts/
│   ├── validate-outline.py                  # every scene has pov/goal/conflict/turn/outcome; every entity exists in bible; beat % sanity
│   ├── build-context-pack.py                # style.md + scene card + prev-scene tail (~1.5k words) + running.md + sheets + facts for named entities
│   ├── lint-scene.sh                        # wordcount band, always-fix list, per-1k rate limits, sentence-length CV, opener distribution, trigram repeats, name list
│   ├── extract-facts.py                     # who knows what, objects, injuries, locations → facts-ledger.yaml
│   ├── baseline-author.py                   # runs lint on the author's own samples to set per-author thresholds
│   └── book-metrics.py                      # chapter-length CV, % chapters ending on one-line paragraph / abstract noun, dialogue ratio per scene
└── templates/                               # character.md, scene-card.md, chapter-summary.md, critique-note.md, author-note.md
```

**Book state** lives in the author's repository as Markdown and YAML so the author can edit any file directly, following Sudowrite's bible, InkOS's projections and RecurrentGPT's editable memory. `book/bible/` holds `premise.md` (logline, promise, theme as a controlling-idea sentence, counter-idea, ending; `## ⚓ Anchored` sections are immutable to agents), `style.md` (2–3 author sample passages, POV/tense, rate limits, sentence-rhythm notes, the name list), `characters/<slug>.md` (Want/Need/Lie/Truth/Ghost, voice tics with a NEVER-SAYS list, arc beats, a knowledge ledger of what they know and when), `world/`, `timeline.md`, and the derived, rebuildable `facts-ledger.yaml`. `book/outline/` holds `acts.md`, `chapters.md` and the scene manifest `scenes.yaml` (id, chapter, pov, location, time, goal, conflict, turn, outcome, value shift, status, word target; for mysteries also `clues_planted`, `clues_paid`, `suspect_lies`). `book/manuscript/chNN/sNN.md` is one file per scene with status frontmatter (`draft|revised|locked`). `book/summaries/` holds 150–300-word chapter summaries and a rolling `running.md`; `book/critiques/chNN/<agent>-<date>.md` holds evidence-citing notes with proposed diff hunks; `book/progress.md` is the resumable checkpoint with next action, open threads with admission dates, and a decisions log.

**The loop.** Stage 1: `story-architect` writes `premise.md` and `acts.md`, offering at least five divergent endings and premises; the author edits and anchors. Stage 2: `character-designer` and `worldbuilder` run in parallel (independent files); the author reviews. Stage 3: `outliner` writes `chapters.md` and `scenes.yaml`; `validate-outline.py` rejects any scene without a turn, any entity not in the bible, and any outline where the midpoint, pinch points and 75% low are missing or the climax lands before 80%; the outliner fixes until it passes; the author approves. Stage 4, drafting, runs *sequentially per POV thread*: `build-context-pack.py` composes roughly 8–15k tokens (style samples, scene card, previous scene tail, running summary, relevant sheets, facts for named entities); `scene-drafter` writes one scene; `lint-scene.sh` fires on write and, on failure, the drafter revises once; `extract-facts.py` updates the ledger and `continuity-editor` records observations, not blocks; after each chapter a summary is written and `progress.md` updated; the author checks in per chapter (or per act on autopilot). Stage 5, critique, runs as a *parallel blind panel*: N `reader-simulator` personas, `line-editor`, `genre-specialist`, `de-ai-editor` and `continuity-editor` each write a critique file with quoted evidence and diff hunks, never seeing each other's notes or process files; `judge` merges, dedupes and ranks, scoring by the minimum rubric dimension rather than the mean. Stage 6: the author accepts or rejects notes in `critiques/`; `revise` applies only accepted diffs and re-runs lint and continuity; stages 5–6 loop at most K times or until no critical findings remain. Stage 7 is a manuscript-level pass per act and per book (structure, arcs, endings, setup/payoff ledger, denouement length), then export.

**Author checkpoints** are enforced by five mechanisms rather than by hoping the model asks: every stage ends by writing files and stopping; `## ⚓ Anchored` headings that reviewers must not contradict and the canon guard hook will not let agents edit; `disable-model-invocation: true` on all side-effecting skills; diff-style critique output so the author sees exactly what would change; and `progress.md` as the resumable checkpoint surfaced by the `SessionStart` hook. Model routing follows Anthropic's cost finding: Opus or Fable for the architect, outliner, drafter and judge; Sonnet for editors, the genre specialist, the de-AI editor and reader personas; Haiku for continuity extraction. Reviewers get read-only tools. During long drafting runs set `subagentPromptCacheTtl: 1h` so the shared prefix (craft skills plus style) stays cached ([Agent teams](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)). Expect the full pipeline to cost on the order of **10–15x** a single-pass draft, so default to "draft one chapter, critique one chapter" rather than whole-book autopilot.

**Anti-homogenisation is built in rather than bolted on.** Author voice samples sit in every drafter prompt near the end, because example dialogue loses influence in long contexts ([voice thread](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qvkkha/snapshots/2026-02-06T18%3A11%3A41.924807Z)). `book-metrics.py` measures sentence-opener distribution, trigram repetition and dialogue-to-narration ratio, the exact metrics on which machine stories failed in Agents' Room ([Agents' Room](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02603v2)). Idea stages produce five premises, three endings and three scene approaches for the author to choose among. The scene manifest requires a distinct turn per scene and the outline validator requires a subplot, a morally compromised protagonist beat, at least one resolution not driven by the protagonist and at least one thread left open, directly targeting the StoryScope gaps. A reader persona is asked to predict the ending at the midpoint as a predictability check. The de-AI pass is restricted to proposing diffs against a per-author baseline so it cannot flatten voice further, and its replacement suggestions draw from randomised sets so the fix does not become a new fingerprint.

**Phased build plan.** Phase 1 (foundation, one to two weeks): `plugin.json`, `novel-start`, the book-tree templates, `craft-*` knowledge skills condensed from the beat map, scene test, character audit and prose checklist above, and `genre/references/mystery.md` plus `mystery-nordic-finnish.md` only; validate with `claude plugin validate --strict` and develop with `claude --plugin-dir ./novelsmith`. Phase 2 (outline-first core, two weeks): `story-architect`, `character-designer`, `worldbuilder`, `outliner`, `validate-outline.py`, the canon guard hook; this is where the evidence says the quality lives. Phase 3 (drafting loop, two weeks): `build-context-pack.py`, `scene-drafter`, `lint-scene.sh` with the three-tier taxonomy and `baseline-author.py`, `extract-facts.py`, `continuity-editor`, `SessionStart` status hook. Phase 4 (critique and revise, two weeks): the blind panel agents, `judge` with a pairwise length-debiased rubric, `revise` applying accepted diffs only, `SubagentStop` output check; build at least three evaluations before extensive documentation and test the drafter on Haiku, Sonnet and Opus as Anthropic's skill guidance prescribes ([Skill best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices)). Phase 5 (breadth): the remaining genre reference files in the order thriller, romance (with romantic suspense), fantasy, science fiction, horror, literary, historical, kidlit, erotica, blends; `book-metrics.py`; optional `workflows/` for the reader panel and continuity sweep; a `.claude-plugin/marketplace.json` for installation. Phase 6 (calibration): write one Finnish crime novella end to end, log every hand-edit, and promote recurring edits into permanent rules in `style.md` and the lint lists, which is the loop Craft found actually changed model behaviour.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. "Sounding AI-written" is not a vocabulary condition that a banned-word list can cure; it is a narrative condition (tidy plots, early climaxes, stated themes, protagonists who are never wrong, body-cue emotion in place of plain feeling, conflicts that dissolve) that happens to be the same condition editors have always called weak craft. That means the de-AI layer and the craft layer are one layer, and the most effective anti-AI measure in the plugin is the outline validator that refuses a manifest without a subplot, a compromised protagonist and an open thread, not the regex that counts em dashes. It also means the Nordic and Finnish crime conventions are unusually well matched to the task: ambiguous endings, spare laconic dialogue, humour inside gloom, a criminal's POV and a season that acts as antagonist are exactly the moves a frontier model avoids by default, so a genre skill that enforces them pushes the output away from the statistical centre as a side effect of making it a better Finnish mystery.

The honest limits are that no published study evaluates a full AI-drafted novel, no Claude Code novel plugin has a measured quality record, all numeric lint thresholds are practitioner choices rather than corpus-validated norms, and LLM judges remain unreliable for absolute creative quality. The design compensates by never letting a model's claim stand in for a file on disk, by making every reviewer cite quoted evidence, by keeping the author's selection between divergent options as the point where diversity re-enters, and by treating the author's own pre-AI prose as the calibration standard. The plugin's job is to make the author's judgement cheaper to apply, not to replace it, and the build should be judged by how many hand-edits per chapter it removes over time.
